Company introduction

PKYDrip develops modular irrigation and fertigation automation for agricultural and landscape projects. Work is organised around system structure—pump management, multi-zone sequencing, fertigation channels, sensors, and optional wireless or remote layers—rather than isolated devices.

Typical partners include irrigation contractors, system integrators, distributors, and OEM buyers who need defined control boundaries, commissioning documentation, and paths to scale from a single site to multi-zone or multi-site rollouts.

  • Control architectures integrating pumps, valves, sensors, and communication
  • Staged upgrades: timed control → zoning → wireless expansion → fertigation where required
  • Regional power and protocol options (e.g. 110V / 240V / 415V; RS485/Modbus where specified)
  • Applications: orchards, greenhouses, open-field farms, solar-remote sites, landscape water management

Irrigation retrofit & automation

Existing sites often retain bore pumps, tanks, mains, and manual valves. Retrofit scope is control and zoning: controller logic, pump rules, automated valve sequencing, schedules, and records—without replacing serviceable pipe networks.

  • Pump interlocks and irrigation windows matched to available flow
  • Zone sequencing when simultaneous demand exceeds supply
  • Optional wireless valve layers where trenching is costly

Control cabinets & pump control

Engineered cabinets and panels consolidate power distribution, I/O, and application logic for pump start/stop, valve drivers, operator interface, and field termination. Builds are aligned to project electrical standards, enclosure rating, and commissioning requirements.

  • Relay / contactor interfaces matched to existing motor or starter arrangements
  • Pressure, flow, or level inputs when the specification requires closed-loop behaviour
  • Documentation for integrator commissioning and handover
References Pump & valve control system · Control panel · Automation & control

Fertigation systems

Water–fertilizer integration provides scheduled or closed-loop nutrient delivery tied to irrigation events. Channel count, injection method, and EC/pH regulation are defined per crop, water quality, and greenhouse or field layout.

  • Multi-channel dosing and mixing; interlocks with pump and zone schedules
  • EC/pH monitoring and limits where closed-loop control is required
  • Coordination with main irrigation controller and pump run conditions
References Water–fertilizer integration · Fertigation category

Wireless / LoRa irrigation

Control pumps and valves across the farm without running cable to every point. A central control cabinet and pump station work with solar-powered wireless valve units in the field—suited to orchards, open fields, and greenhouse expansions.

  • Manage irrigation from the pump room or remotely—fewer long cable runs
  • Solar-powered valve controllers for distant zones
  • Add zones over time without rewiring the whole property
References LoRa wireless control · Open-field irrigation · LoRa system overview

Applications

PKYDrip systems are used in greenhouses, open fields, and upgrade projects where clear pump and zone control matters.

Typical deployment patterns

  • Greenhouse: fertigation tie-in, valve expansion, climate-adjacent inputs as specified
  • Open field: solar nodes, long laterals, constrained simultaneous zones
  • Retrofit: keep existing hydraulics; upgrade pump and valve control first
